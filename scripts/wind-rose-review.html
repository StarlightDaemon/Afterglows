Wind Rose

A record of where the wind comes from. Each observation adds to a compass-direction branch. Longer branches mean more observations; the three colors separate wind speeds.

Read the branches

A southwest branch means wind from the southwest. The rings count 1, 3 and 5 observations.

Read the colors

Gold: 1–<3 m/s. Teal: 3–<6 m/s. Coral: 6+ m/s. Speed bands stay in this order.

Calm stays separate

Two of the 20 synthetic readings are calm, below 1 m/s. They receive no compass direction.

This is an illustrative dataset, not weather from a real station. The completed rose holds before replaying: southwest accounts for 5/20 observations (25%), west for 4/20 (20%), and calm for 2/20 (10%).

Definition and sources

A meteorological wind rose summarizes the frequency of wind from different directions and its speed ranges at a location. This study uses a fixed count scale while the synthetic records accumulate.
